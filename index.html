Simulation instructions
Methods and data sources

Mass fractions are combined by the mixture rule. The optimizer holds density, thickness, energy and binder fraction fixed, then assigns mass to the allowed elements in descending order of their mass attenuation coefficients up to their specified limits. Atom counts are converted using approximate atomic masses derived from NIST Z/A values; isotopes and bonds are not specified. Density in preset mode defaults to an ideal additive-volume estimate, assuming no porosity; replace it with a measured value. Custom mode requires a bulk density. Transmission is exp(-μ/ρ × density × thickness), for a uniform sheet at normal incidence. Lead equivalence is energy-specific.

Atomic and particle arrangements cannot be inferred from element names alone. Modeling them requires a specified chemical structure or spatial geometry and a suitable simulation; this homogeneous model does not resolve them.

Flexibility, toxicity, manufacturing feasibility and dose are not predicted. NIST tables: element attenuation (Z=1–92), element constants, and polyethylene.
